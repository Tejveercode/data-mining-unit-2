## The unit in one paragraph

Real data is dirty, huge and redundant. **Pre-processing** (Ch. 2.1–2.2) makes it *trustworthy* (cleaning), *unified* (integration), *compact* (reduction) and *usable* (transformation, discretization) — every technique answers one of four questions: is it correct, is it unified, is it too big, is it in usable form. **Association mining** (Ch. 2.3) then answers the question the data was prepared for: *which things occur together, how often, and better than chance?* Support measures how often, confidence measures how reliably, lift measures whether it is more than coincidence; Apriori finds the frequent itemsets level by level using the anti-monotonic property, FP-Growth does it with two scans and a compressed tree, and rule generation turns the itemsets into strong rules.

## How to study this in three passes

**Pass 1 — understand (≈2 h).** Read notes 01→13 in order. Do not memorise. Each note opens with a one-paragraph "why this exists" — that paragraph is the answer to most "explain" questions.

**Pass 2 — practise (≈2 h).** Work notes 11 and 08 with the answers covered. Apriori and entropy numericals are formula-driven and repeat exactly.

**Pass 3 — retrieve (≈1 h, then 10 min daily).** Cover the cheat sheet and recall from memory. Work the question bank out loud. Spaced recall beats re-reading.
